## Slide 24 — Background: Why Diffusion/Flow Policies are Slow

### Layout / レイアウト
- **Title:** Why diffusion/flow policies? And why are they slow?
- **Top half (the good):**
  - 🎯 **Multi-modal action distributions** — many valid trajectories for one task
  - 📉 MSE-trained policies average over them → degenerate behaviors
- **Bottom half (the bad):**
  - ⏱️ Sample in **action-sequence space** $\mathcal{A}^T$ — "trajectory of trajectories"
  - Start from pure Gaussian noise → must finish full denoising loop
  - 🚫 No actions can reach the robot until the loop completes
  - All intermediate trajectories are discarded — wasteful
  - Streaming Diffusion Policy (Høeg et al. 2025, ICRA) partially fixes this with a rolling buffer of increasing noise levels, but pays an upfront initialization cost

### Talking script — English
> First, why have diffusion- and flow-based policies become popular? Because they naturally represent multi-modal action distributions. For many manipulation tasks — say, picking up a cup — multiple trajectories are equally valid. MSE-trained policies tend to average them into degenerate behaviors, while diffusion policies preserve the modes. But there is a cost: conventional diffusion/flow policies sample in the *action-sequence* space $\mathcal{A}^T$ — they generate a "trajectory of trajectories", starting from pure Gaussian noise. Every intermediate trajectory along the denoising path is discarded, which is wasteful, and worse, no action can be sent to the robot until the full denoising loop completes. Streaming Diffusion Policy by Høeg et al. partially mitigates this with a rolling buffer of increasing noise levels across the prediction horizon, but it requires an upfront buffer initialization and still incurs noticeable latency.

### Talking script — 日本語
> まず、なぜDiffusion/Flowベースのポリシーが人気なのか。理由は、マルチモーダルなアクション分布を自然に表現できるからです。「コップを掴む」のようなタスクでは、等しく妥当な軌道が複数存在する。MSE損失で訓練するとそれを平均化して変な行動になりますが、Diffusionポリシーはモードを保てる。しかし代償もあります。従来のDiffusion/Flowポリシーは「アクション系列空間 $\mathcal{A}^T$」でサンプリングする。つまり「軌道の軌道」を生成していて、しかも純粋なGaussianノイズから始める。denoising中の中間軌道は全部捨てられるという無駄もあるし、もっと深刻なのは、denoisingループが終わるまでロボットには1つのアクションも送れないこと。Streaming Diffusion Policy（Høegら, ICRA 2025）はrolling bufferを使って遅延を一部緩和しますが、bufferの初期化に前払いコストが必要で、根本的な解決ではありません。

## Slide 25 — Key Idea: Action Trajectory = Flow Trajectory

### Layout / レイアウト
- **Title:** Key idea: align *flow time* with *execution time*
- **Main visual:** Paper Fig. 1 (left: conventional in $\mathcal{A}^T$; right: SFP in $\mathcal{A}$)
- **Annotations:**
  - **Left (conventional):** "Trajectory of trajectories" — vertical = diffusion/flow time, horizontal = execution time. Must climb the full vertical axis before sending anything.
  - **Right (SFP):** A *single* action trajectory in $\mathcal{A}$, where **flow time = execution time**. Stream as you integrate.
- **Highlighted insight:** *"Don't sample trajectories — flow through one."*
- **Bonus note:** Network input/output dimension shrinks from $\mathcal{A}^T \to \mathcal{A}$ → smaller GPU footprint, faster training

### Talking script — English
> Here is the central idea, and it is beautifully simple. Look at the figure. On the left, the conventional diffusion/flow policy: the vertical axis is diffusion or flow time, the horizontal axis is execution time. You have to climb all the way up the vertical axis — the full denoising loop — before any action can be sent. You're sampling a "trajectory of trajectories". On the right, SFP: there is just *one* action trajectory in the action space, and flow time is aligned with execution time. As the ODE solver integrates forward in flow time, it's also moving forward in execution time, so every newly integrated action can be streamed directly to the robot. The slogan: don't sample trajectories — flow through one. As a side benefit, the network's input and output dimension shrinks from $\mathcal{A}^T$ to just $\mathcal{A}$, so training is faster and the GPU footprint is smaller.

### Talking script — 日本語
> 中心アイデアはとてもシンプルで、図を見れば一目瞭然です。左が従来のDiffusion/Flowポリシー。縦軸がdiffusion timeまたはflow time、横軸が実行時間で、まず縦軸を最後まで登り切らないと、つまりdenoisingループを最後まで回さないと、ロボットには何も送れない。「軌道の軌道」をサンプリングしている状態ですね。右がSFP。アクション空間にあるのはたった1本のアクション軌道で、しかもflow timeが実行時間と一致しています。だからODE solverがflow timeで前に進むと、同時に実行時間でも前に進むので、新しく積分されたアクションはそのままロボットに流せる。スローガンは「軌道をサンプリングするな、1本を流れろ」。副次的な利点として、ネットワークの入出力次元が $\mathcal{A}^T$ から $\mathcal{A}$ に縮むので、訓練が速くなりGPUメモリも少なくて済みます。

## Slide 26 — Method: Stabilizing Velocity Field

### Layout / レイアウト
- **Title:** Method: stabilizing conditional velocity field
- **Construction (per demo $\xi$):**
$$v_\xi(a, t) = \underbrace{\dot{\xi}(t)}_{\text{trajectory velocity}} - \underbrace{k(a - \xi(t))}_{\text{stabilization (P-control)}}, \quad p^0_\xi(a) = \mathcal{N}(a \mid \xi(0), \sigma_0^2)$$
- **Resulting marginal (Theorem 1):**
$$p_\xi(a \mid t) = \mathcal{N}(a \mid \xi(t),\, \sigma_0^2 e^{-2kt})$$
- **Geometric picture (right side):** Exponentially-shrinking Gaussian "tube" around the demo trajectory
- **Note:** Uses $\dot{\xi}(t)$ as a learning signal — *not* exploited by conventional diffusion/flow policies
- **Theoretical motivation:** Block et al. (NeurIPS 2023): stabilizing low-level controllers reduce distribution shift and tighten imitation learning bounds

### Talking script — English
> The method itself. For every demonstration trajectory ξ in the training set, the authors analytically construct a stabilizing conditional velocity field. The first term, ξ-dot of t, is the velocity along the demonstration. The second term, minus k times (a minus ξ(t)), is a proportional stabilization term — a simple P-controller pulling deviations back toward the demonstration. The initial action distribution is a narrow Gaussian centered at the demonstration's start point, ξ(0), with small variance sigma-zero-squared. Theorem 1 in the paper shows that the induced per-timestep marginal is a Gaussian tube around the demonstration, whose width shrinks exponentially as e to the minus 2kt. Two important remarks. First, this construction uses the time derivative ξ-dot of the demonstration, which conventional diffusion/flow policies do *not* use — SFP exploits the temporal structure of the trajectory. Second, the stabilization is motivated by prior theory: Block et al. at NeurIPS 2023 showed that low-level stabilizing controllers reduce distribution shift and tighten imitation-learning generalization bounds.

### Talking script — 日本語
> 手法に入ります。訓練セットの各実演軌道 ξ に対して、著者らは「安定化付きの条件速度場」を解析的に構成します。第1項 $\dot{\xi}(t)$ は実演に沿った速度。第2項の $-k(a-\xi(t))$ は比例項で、要するにP制御で実演軌道に向かって偏差を引き戻す働きをします。初期アクション分布は、実演の始点 $\xi(0)$ を中心とする狭いガウス分布です。論文のTheorem 1により、これによって誘導される各時刻の周辺分布は実演の周りのGaussianチューブ状になり、その幅が $e^{-2kt}$ で指数的に狭まっていく。重要な点が2つ。1つ目、この構成は実演の時間微分 $\dot{\xi}(t)$ を使っていて、これは従来のDiffusion/Flowポリシーが使っていない情報です。SFPは軌道の時間構造を活用しているわけです。2つ目、安定化項にはきちんとした理論的根拠があって、NeurIPS 2023のBlockらが「低レベル安定化制御器は分布シフトを減らし、模倣学習の汎化バウンドを締める」ことを示しています。

## Slide 27 — Training & Inference

### Layout / レイアウト
- **Title:** Training & Inference
- **Top: Training (flow matching loss, Eq. 4)**
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CFM}}(v, p_\mathcal{D}) = \mathbb{E}_{(h, \xi) \sim p_\mathcal{D},\, t \sim U[0,1],\, a \sim p_\xi(a|t)} \left\| v(a, t \mid h) - v_\xi(a, t) \right\|_2^2$$
- 📌 *Flow-matching theorem (Lipman et al. 2023) → learned marginal matches the data-induced marginal at every $t$, so multi-modality is preserved.*
- **Bottom: Inference (Alg. 2)**
  - At test time, set $\sigma_0 = 0$ → deterministic policy (multi-modality already in network weights)
  - ODE solver integrates **autoregressively**: each action depends only on previously computed ones
  - Each newly integrated action → **streamed directly to controller**
  - **State imitation bonus:** initialize integration from *measured* robot state → proprioceptive feedback enters via both $h$ and $a_0$

### Talking script — English
> Training is standard conditional flow matching: regress the network onto the analytical velocity field we just constructed. By the flow-matching theorem of Lipman et al., the minimizer's marginal action distribution at every t exactly matches the training-data marginal, so multi-modality is preserved even when many demonstrations are aggregated. At inference, the authors set sigma-zero to zero, making the policy deterministic — the multi-modality lives in the learned network weights, not in test-time noise. This is the same trick ACT uses. The ODE solver integrates autoregressively: each new action depends only on previously computed ones. So as soon as an action is integrated, it can be streamed to the controller. There's also a clever extension the authors call "state imitation": for tasks defined in robot configuration space, you can initialize the integration from the *measured* robot state, not just from the predicted last action. This means proprioception enters the policy twice — once through the observation history h, and once through the initial action a-zero — which significantly reduces accumulated error.

### Talking script — 日本語
> 訓練は標準的な条件付きflow matchingで、ニューラルネットを今構成した解析的な速度場に回帰させます。LipmanらのFlow-matching定理により、最適解の周辺アクション分布は各時刻tで訓練データの周辺分布と厳密に一致する。だから多数の実演を集約してもマルチモーダル性が保たれます。推論時は $\sigma_0=0$ にしてポリシーを決定的にします。マルチモーダル性は学習済みの重みの中に既に入っているので、テスト時にノイズを足す必要はない。ACTと同じ発想です。ODE solverは自己回帰的に積分するので、各アクションは過去のアクションだけに依存する。だから積分されたアクションは即座にコントローラへ流せる。さらに著者らが「state imitation」と呼ぶ拡張も面白くて、ロボットのconfiguration空間で定義されたタスクでは、積分の初期値を「予測された前のアクション」ではなく「実測されたロボット状態」から始められる。こうすると、proprioceptionが2か所、つまり観測履歴 $h$ と初期アクション $a_0$ の両方からポリシーに入る形になって、累積誤差を大きく減らせます。

## Slide 28 — A Subtle Caveat (and Why It's Actually a Feature)

### Layout / レイアウト
- **Title:** Caveat: marginals ≠ joint
- **Two-column layout:**
  - **Left (what SFP guarantees):**
    ✅ Marginal action distribution at every $t$ matches training data
  - **Right (what SFP does NOT guarantee):**
    ❌ Joint distribution over **entire trajectories**
    (→ may compose new shapes, e.g. "S" + "Ƨ" → "Ɛ" or "3" — see paper Fig. 4)
- **Below (highlighted):**
  - *"For quasi-static manipulation, this is a feature, not a bug."*
  - Policy can compose new valid trajectories from segments of demos → fewer demos needed
  - **Local constraints provably respected:**
    - Joint/position limits (Sec. 9.3)
    - Convex velocity constraints (Sec. 9.4) — e.g. $\dot{\xi}(t) \in [v_{\min}, v_{\max}]$

### Talking script — English
> One subtle caveat that the authors are very explicit about. SFP matches the marginal action distribution at every timestep, but it does not necessarily match the joint distribution over the entire trajectory. The paper shows a nice toy example with two intersecting demonstrations of shapes "S" and reverse-"S"; SFP can sample novel composite shapes like "Ɛ" or "3" that weren't in the training data. The authors argue — and I find this convincing — that for quasi-static manipulation this is a *feature* rather than a bug, because it lets the policy compose novel valid behaviors from training segments, reducing the number of demonstrations needed. Importantly, while *global* joint-distribution constraints are lost, they prove that *local* constraints are still respected: arbitrary position constraints like joint limits, and convex velocity constraints like minimum and maximum joint velocities, are preserved by the learned flow. So you trade joint-distribution fidelity for compositional generality — a reasonable trade for most physical tasks.

### Talking script — 日本語
> ひとつ細かいですが大事な留意点を共有します。著者らも明示しています。SFPが保証するのは「各時刻における周辺アクション分布の一致」であって、「軌道全体の同時分布の一致」ではありません。論文には綺麗なトイ例があって、「S」字と逆「S」字の2つの交差する実演を訓練すると、SFPは訓練データに無い「Ɛ」や「3」のような合成形状もサンプリングし得る。著者らの主張は、準静的マニピュレーションではこれは「バグではなく特徴」だ、というもので、実演セグメントから新しい妥当な行動を合成できるので必要な実演数を減らせる、という利点になる。重要なのは、*大域的*な同時分布の制約は失われますが、*局所的*な制約は保たれることが理論的に証明されている点です。具体的には、関節限界などの任意の位置制約（Sec. 9.3）、そして最小・最大関節速度のような凸な速度制約（Sec. 9.4）は学習後のフローでも維持されます。つまり、同時分布の忠実度を譲って合成的な汎化を得ているという trade-off ですが、ほとんどの物理タスクでは妥当な交換だと思います。

  - SFP additionally allows **async parallel generation + execution** → even tighter sensorimotor loop
- **Closing summary of 3 papers:**
  - 🎯 Common thread: tightening the loop between policy and the physical world

### Talking script — English
> The real-world experiments use a Franka Research 3 arm with a RealSense D435f camera, on two tasks: reaching and picking up an apple, and reorienting a block to a vertical goal orientation. Qualitatively, SFP produces noticeably smoother and faster motions than Diffusion Policy — videos are on the project website. Here is a subtle but important observation: the smoothness advantage does not come *only* from lower average latency. It comes from the fact that SFP emits actions at a *uniform* interval as the ODE integrates. Diffusion policies, even fast variants, must complete the entire denoising loop before any action is sent, which produces a stop-and-go execution pattern even when average latency looks similar. Beyond that, SFP enables asynchronous parallel generation and execution, allowing an even tighter sensorimotor loop. So uniform emission and async overlap are both real advantages, independent of raw speed. And that wraps up the three papers — the common thread across them is tightening the loop between the learned policy and the physical world. Thank you very much for listening — happy to take questions.
